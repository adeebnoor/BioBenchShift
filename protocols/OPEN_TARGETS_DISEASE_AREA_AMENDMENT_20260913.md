# Open Targets disease-area aggregation amendment — 13 September 2026

## Status

This amendment is committed before any Open Targets disease-area outcome for the competing SVD and NeuralMF queues is generated or inspected. It implements the disease-area context anticipated in `BIOLOGICAL_CONSEQUENCE_AMENDMENT_20260913.md`.

Frozen baseline: `1298e4c83ff4fa488a784d52219d20e706118a9e`.

## Data release and mapping

Use Open Targets Platform **26.06** through the public GraphQL endpoint. Record the returned API/data version when available. Convert NCBI Entrez Gene IDs to Ensembl gene IDs using the current frozen g:Profiler conversion service version used by the pathway analysis, with organism `hsapiens`, target namespace `ENSG`, and numeric namespace `ENTREZGENE_ACC`. Report mapping coverage and any one-to-many mappings; when multiple ENSG mappings remain, select the lexicographically first ENSG deterministically and retain the ambiguity count.

## Queue definitions

Use exactly the Top-100, Top-500 and Top-1,000 unique target-gene sets already generated by the preregistered biological-consequence analysis from the same five-initialization SVD and NeuralMF full-candidate ensembles. No target may be added or removed based on Open Targets results.

## Association definition

For each mapped target, retrieve **direct** target-disease associations exposed on the Open Targets target endpoint and their overall association scores. Open Targets association scores are treated as ranking weights, not confidence probabilities.

For every target and every high-level Open Targets therapeutic area:

1. collect all direct diseases assigned to that therapeutic area;
2. take the **maximum overall association score** among those diseases as that target's score for the area, preventing a target with many near-duplicate disease terms from dominating solely by term multiplicity;
3. if a target has positive scores in multiple therapeutic areas, normalize its therapeutic-area vector to sum to one so that every annotated target contributes total mass one;
4. average the normalized target vectors within each model/K queue.

Targets with no mapped ENSG identifier or no direct Open Targets association contribute no disease-area mass; their counts are reported explicitly. The primary comparison is conditional on targets with at least one disease-area annotation.

## Prespecified comparisons

For K = 100, 500 and 1,000 report:

- Entrez-to-Ensembl mapping coverage per queue;
- number and fraction of targets with at least one direct disease-area annotation;
- normalized therapeutic-area distribution for each selected-model queue;
- Jensen–Shannon divergence between the SVD and NeuralMF therapeutic-area distributions;
- overlap coefficient of the five highest-mass therapeutic areas;
- the complete, unfiltered therapeutic-area table rather than a manually selected subset.

## Bootstrap uncertainty

For each K, independently bootstrap targets within each queue with replacement for **2,000** replicates, repeat the equal-target aggregation, and report the median and 95% percentile interval of Jensen–Shannon divergence. The random seed is fixed to `20260913 + K`.

## Interpretation boundary

This analysis asks whether benchmark-selected queues allocate attention across different disease areas. It does not validate an individual drug-target pair, does not show clinical efficacy, and does not imply that a higher Open Targets association score is biological truth. Open Targets integrates heterogeneous public evidence and may overlap in provenance with other resources; it is interpretive context rather than an independent pair-level endpoint.
